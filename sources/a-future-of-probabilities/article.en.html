Update: Part II of this article was published on 3/29/2023.

Just like the dawn of Desktop Internet era (some called it web1) and the Mobile Internet era (some called it web2, with social media and user generated contents), this current AI heat wave across the globe brings the same feeling of opportunities and excitement. A new killer app is unfolding in front of our eyes. We are seeing another tectonic shift in the underlying computing platform for the world. The AI Internet era (with a lot of AI-generated contents), has arrived. As Andrew Ng put it, we are in a boiling hot summer of AI.

Everything will change. Unless you are Gen Z, it is very easy to relate now if you reflect back on how Desktop Internet changed the world and Mobile Internet did it a second time.

The declaration of web3 by Chris Dixon now looks a little ambitious in its scoping. However, having been a “web3” entrepreneur myself, I still firmly believe in the unique technological value of blockchain and the important role it can and will play in the AI Internet era. Not only does its innate capability to solve trust provide many crucial utilities for the new era, its DNA of anti-establishment and affirming individual freedom, ownership and expression is sorely needed in a world increasingly run by machines and big corporations that can afford them.

Tim Berners-Lee, the inventor of the World Wide Web, with James Hendler and Ora Lassila, envisioned a semantic web as the next iteration of the Internet more than 20 years ago. It looks like their vision of personal agents and business agents working together on our behalf, may well be realized on the AI Internet finally. Only now do we know that these agents are running on top of LLMs built with a superset of human knowledge.

Just like the previous two paradigm shifts, many things will be reimagined and redefined, to use the two keywords popularized by Mary Meeker in the Mobile Internet era. The entire technology stack and application stack are being reimagined and redefined right now.

OpenAI has come out with the first productized platform on which ChatGPT and many emerging innovative products are built. Competing platforms are brewing and will delight us again in their own unique ways, be it cultural understanding, domain understanding, emotional understanding, with nuances and complexities of different practices, interactions and customs.

Middleware layer, which include prompt engineering tools, fine tuning tools, moderation tools, data ingestion and integration tools, process orchestration tools, MLOps and LLMOps tools, is forming. And the startups that build them are popping up every day, or are being called for. The exciting models and tools that can be and will be built at this layer will power the many personalized and customized agents in the years to come.

So are horizontal applications startups, esp. in the area of productivity, creativity, knowledge management, social networking, e-commerce, and CRM, which are now in the hundreds. There are still holes to plug and we can expect to see more coming. And just like driving, we can foresee that each app will likely have its autonomy levels defined by its industry’s equivalent of SAE. Right now they are at L0 going to L1, and I cannot wait to see what L2, L3, L4, even L5 will look like in every single app that targets a particular job and human endeavor. We will have an app-induced content explosion generated by AI that at some point we as human will have no choice but to have a personal agent and family agent to help us sift through all the information and noise that is coming at us and auto-summarize for us, even take actions on our behalf. Unlike the recommendation algorithms of today, these agents, as well as the models behind them and the personal data used to train them, must be and will be owned and controlled by us. A lot of details are yet to be worked out and blockchain can definitely play a role here. AI trust and safety will become an increasingly important and mainstream topic.

Soon vertical applications will come out for the different sectors and industries which will inspire a whole host of domain specific models, that in a strange way, will probably require the help of domain experts to train them, in order to partly or even completely replace the experts. The definition of a domain expert or a subject matter expert, will no longer be domain knowledge or even domain experience. This also applies to the technology sector that includes the software industry of which I am a part. I wonder what lies at the core of being a software engineer, a technical manager, an architect or an executive. I also wonder what our education system especially computer science curricula should look like in order to produce the skilled workforce ready for our future economy and society.

As part of the ecosystem, the AI Internet also provides a huge technology driver that will spurt a new round of business growth for consulting companies. We will see them rebrand their cloud, data science and analytics expertise as AI expertise and rush to build it. As for the industries, this round of technology innovation is not just incremental that delivers productivity boost or cost saving in the near term. For any company, this round of innovation will incur the reinvention of its business and redefinition of its organization and the jobs within it. Success will belong to those companies who have an articulate vision on its role and core value proposition in a future new world of automatons within and without, and who can execute to that vision. The consulting industry will have an important thought-leadership and bridge role to play while the industry itself undergoes its own repurposing and reinvention. More importantly, the creation of organizational agents in different domains and the process orchestration of the different agents (including those created for individuals) to automate and accomplish different real-world tasks, and the productization along the way, will help advance the state of the art.

The role of humans and machines will be redefined. The elusive concept of AGI and singularity seems to be here now. This is a shift that even industry insiders did not foresee would come this fast. But it is happening. What was deemed impossible or science fiction is now possible and reality. Opportunities and startups are everywhere. It feels like 1995 and 2008. The transformer architecture has indeed brought about a sustained period of a transformative change of which we are just at the very beginning. Along the way, as Sam Altman pointed out, AGI would also come with serious risk of misuse, drastic accidents, and societal disruption. Coming from his vantage point, the “boundless downside and boundless upside” comment should not be taken lightly.

Human curiosity and ingenuity, economic incentives and network effects will continue to drive this technology forward regardless of such risks. Ready or not, this is a heat wave that we as human race have no choice but to embrace and manage. We have finally come to confront the age-old philosophical questions on the co-existence of man and intelligent machine, and the matrix.
