TL;DR

In October 2026, OpenAI's release of AI-generated mathematical research, and the mathematical community's pushback, put one word back in focus: permissionless. Blockchain and AI are driving the same shift from two directions. Blockchain lets strangers transact and collaborate under open rules instead of relying on trusted intermediaries; AI lets individuals build products, tools and even research without first winning the approval of investors, software vendors or institutions. Permissionlessness does not abolish expertise or regulation: it redefines authority as something earned through continued contribution. For young people, it means not waiting to be told they are ready. For public policy, it means keeping compute and open networks broadly accessible, so that this freedom does not become the privilege of a few.

In October 2026, a discussion on the All-In Podcast about OpenAI’s mathematical research results and the mathematical community’s response brought one word into sharp focus: permissionless. Literally, it means not requiring permission. The term is hardly new. During the Web3 and blockchain boom several years ago, permissionlessness was almost a defining principle of the industry. Yet hearing it applied in the AI era to mathematical research, and even to the advancement of human knowledge, offers an entirely new perspective. [1]

Blockchain and artificial intelligence may be driving the same transformation from two different directions: more and more activities that once required approval from authorities, institutional endorsement, or substantial resources can now begin without prior permission.

1.When AI Begins to Challenge the Authority of Mathematics#

On October 6, 2026, OpenAI released a collection of mathematical research results generated by its internal frontier AI model, addressing numerous open problems in mathematics and theoretical computer science. According to information released at the time, the model attempted approximately 4,000 problems, produced 372 families of results, and initially published 722 research manuscripts, including work exploring and advancing some long-standing open problems. Especially striking was OpenAI’s description of the computational cost: on average, each result consumed compute equivalent to approximately three hours of ChatGPT Pro thinking. The “three hours” refers to equivalent computational expenditure, not the actual elapsed time needed to solve a problem. Nor should all these manuscripts be treated as complete proofs that have passed independent verification; some findings still require further review and confirmation. [2][3]

Even with those qualifications, the event is profoundly significant. Mathematics is among humanity’s oldest and most rigorous fields of knowledge. Some problems have resisted satisfactory solutions for generations. A doctoral student may devote years to a single question; a professor may build an entire career around one research direction. AI is now participating in the search for proofs, the discovery of methods, and the advancement of research in those very areas.

Traditionally, the frontiers of mathematics have been advanced primarily by formally trained researchers. Academic institutions, research grants, peer review, and professional communities form an essential system for producing knowledge. These mechanisms retain irreplaceable value. But AI systems, which hold neither doctoral degrees nor academic appointments, can now participate directly in investigating frontier problems.

The Mathematical Community Responds

OpenAI’s release sparked controversy among mathematicians. The Association for Human Mathematics (AHM) issued a statement criticizing the manner of publication and expressing concerns about academic standards, research quality, human understanding, and the professional ecosystem of research. Terence Tao reposted the statement on his blog; doing so does not imply that he endorses every argument it contains. [4]

The response from mathematicians cannot simply be dismissed as an effort to protect vested interests. Mathematical proofs demand rigorous verification; academic contributions require clear attribution; and humans still need to understand why an answer is correct. These are genuine and important concerns.

Yet another side of the controversy is equally difficult to ignore. Once a new technology acquires the ability to advance the frontiers of knowledge independently, established scholarly communities no longer hold a monopoly on discovery. Mathematicians can still question proofs, demand verification, and debate how results should enter the body of human knowledge. But refusing to participate can no longer, by itself, prevent AI from exploring further.

Who Gets to Define the Frontiers of Knowledge?

The significance of this transformation may go beyond how knowledge is produced. It challenges an assumption long taken for granted: that the frontiers of a field are defined primarily by its recognized authorities. Traditionally, leading experts, research institutions, and professional communities have largely determined which questions matter, which directions deserve resources, and which results count as breakthroughs.

AI is changing that arrangement. In the future, progress at a discipline’s frontier may be driven not only by its most senior specialists but also by people from other fields working with AI. A physicist may use AI to develop a mathematical idea; a software engineer may identify a hypothesis worth testing in the life sciences; and someone without conventional disciplinary training but with an exceptionally perceptive question may discover what others have overlooked.

This does not mean expertise has lost its value. Verification, interpretation, and the construction of theoretical frameworks still demand deep knowledge. But possessing expertise is not the same as holding an exclusive right to define a field’s frontier.

In the past, authorities largely defined the frontier. In the future, people from many backgrounds, working alongside AI, will increasingly explore that frontier together; established authorities will need to demonstrate their value continuously through understanding, verification, and contribution.

Permissionlessness does more than give more people the right to participate in innovation. It also gives more people an opportunity to help define the future.

2.Permissionlessness Was Not Invented by Web3#

The idea of permissionlessness predates blockchain. The early internet embodied a powerful ethos of open innovation: anyone could build a website, launch a service, or experiment with a new network application without first obtaining approval from a single governing authority. This openness was a major source of the internet’s innovative energy.

In 2014, technology policy scholar Adam Thierer published Permissionless Innovation: The Continuing Case for Comprehensive Technological Freedom, systematically examining the principle that technological innovation should be allowed to proceed without prior authorization. Thierer has also acknowledged that he was not the first person to coin the expression. [5]

Innovation should begin with the freedom to experiment, not with a requirement to obtain permission in advance.

This principle developed gradually as both an engineering culture and an institutional philosophy. Blockchain later gave some aspects of it a more concrete technical implementation.

3.Web3: Transactions and Collaboration Without Reliance on Central Authorities#

In 2009, the Bitcoin network began operating, introducing an organizational model different from traditional financial infrastructure. Previously, transferring value between two distant parties generally required banks, payment networks, or other trusted institutions to maintain records and execute transactions. Bitcoin instead sought to use cryptography, distributed ledgers, and consensus mechanisms to allow parties who do not know one another to transfer value according to publicly defined rules. [6]

Ethereum subsequently extended that idea to smart contracts. On an open public blockchain, participating in the network, validating transactions, and using the protocol generally do not require individualized approval from a central authority. [7]

From Trusting Institutions to Trusting Rules

Traditional business relationships often rely on banks, platforms, lawyers, or guarantors as intermediaries. Smart contracts can encode some agreements as publicly inspectable programs that execute when specified conditions are met—for example, holding transaction funds until agreed conditions trigger payment. They do not eliminate code vulnerabilities, real-world performance risks, or uncertainty about off-chain data. But in some circumstances, they can reduce dependence on a single intermediary.

In the past, we had to trust an institution to carry out an agreement as promised. Today, in some settings, we can rely instead on public, verifiable programmatic rules.

From Platform Control to Transferable Digital Rights

Consider a hotel selling an expensive prepaid accommodation package. Traditionally, transferring that package often requires confirmation from the hotel or booking platform. A hotel could instead link an eligible package entitlement to an NFT and agree, through its contracts and systems, to recognize the on-chain holder. Subject to applicable terms, laws, and redemption requirements, later transfers might then proceed without the hotel manually approving each transaction.

An NFT, however, does not automatically create a real-world right to stay at the hotel. The arrangement works only because of the hotel’s commitment to honor the entitlement, the relevant legal framework, and the verifiable on-chain credential and record. Permissionlessness does not erase real-world obligations; it enables more open transfers within an agreed framework.

4.AI: Making Creation Itself Permissionless#

If blockchain is changing how people collaborate, transact, and establish trust, AI is changing humanity’s ability to create knowledge, products, and services.

Entrepreneurship No Longer Has to Begin with Capital’s Approval

In the past, even someone with an excellent business idea might have had to secure venture capital before hiring engineers, designers, and product managers to turn the concept into a product. Under those conditions, capital effectively acted as a gatekeeper.

AI is reducing the initial barriers to product development. Entrepreneurs can now use coding assistants and AI agents to build prototypes, write front-end and back-end code, and carry out parts of testing and operations. Complex systems still require professional engineering, security reviews, and ongoing maintenance. Even so, founders can increasingly create a product and validate a market before deciding whether to raise capital.

In the past, founders often had to persuade investors before they could acquire the capacity to build. Today, they can increasingly build first and let the product demonstrate its value.

Venture capital will not disappear. It will still help companies scale, expand distribution, and assume major risks. But for some ventures it may shift from a prerequisite for getting started to an optional instrument for growth.

Businesses No Longer Have to Depend on Large Enterprise Software Vendors

Traditionally, improving internal business processes often meant buying enterprise software or paying substantial custom-development fees. For many small and midsize businesses, numerous problems remained unsolved because addressing them was simply too expensive. AI now gives them an opportunity to build smaller tools tailored to their own order management, inventory, customer service, and data-analysis workflows.

Mission-critical systems still require security, privacy, compliance, and maintenance safeguards. But the direction is unmistakable: businesses are moving from buying solutions designed by someone else toward creating solutions around their own needs. Previously, business processes often had to adapt to software; increasingly, software will be able to adapt to business processes.

Scientific Research Is No Longer the Exclusive Domain of a Few Specialized Institutions

Frontier research still demands deep expertise, experimental capabilities, and rigorous validation. Yet AI can assist with reviewing literature, identifying connections, formulating hypotheses, and writing simulations, allowing more people from diverse backgrounds to participate in research. An individual’s research potential will no longer be determined entirely by institutional affiliation, team size, or the resources they can mobilize.

Permissionlessness changes who may participate, not the standards by which claims are judged correct.

5.Why Two Apparently Contradictory Technologies Are Converging#

In a public conversation with Reid Hoffman, Peter Thiel contrasted the libertarian orientation of cryptocurrency with AI’s tendency toward centralized control, highlighting a tension in their underlying power structures. Blockchain seeks open participation and reduced dependence on intermediaries, while training advanced AI models requires enormous computational resources, data, and capital—and may therefore become concentrated in a handful of companies. [8]

But the picture changes when we shift our attention from infrastructure to users. The ability to train frontier models may be concentrated even as the ability to create applications becomes widely distributed. An ordinary developer need not build a data center to write software with the help of a model; a small team need not train a foundation model to build a product.

AI Provides Creative Capacity; Blockchain Provides Infrastructure for Open Collaboration

In a November 2025 conversation with Costis Maglaras, Ben Horowitz, co-founder of the venture capital firm Andreessen Horowitz (a16z), made an observation drawn from the history of computing: each generation of computing has developed through the mutual reinforcement of computers and networks. From mainframes and enterprise networks to personal computers and local-area networks, and then the internet and smartphones, these distinct technologies have created far more value together than either could alone. He then raised a question for the AI era: what kind of network do intelligent agents need? If AI agents move beyond answering questions to purchasing services, receiving payments, and participating in economic activity under user authorization, traditional payment systems designed for human account holders will not always fit their needs. Horowitz argued that cryptocurrency could serve as an internet-native means of exchanging value between agents. Cryptographic technologies could also verify content provenance and identity, help counter the trust problems created by deepfakes, and use public-key systems and zero-knowledge proofs to demonstrate qualifications without requiring parties to disclose all their sensitive data. These possibilities are not yet an industry-wide reality, but they reveal a potential complementarity between AI and blockchain in the future economy: AI expands what we can create, while blockchain explores how we can collaborate, transact, and distribute value under open rules. The former provides new intelligence and creative capacity; the latter may provide open infrastructure for payments, digital ownership, and verifiable collaboration across independent parties. [9]

For example, AI agents operated by different companies could discover services through open protocols, exchange information, complete payments, and use smart contracts to carry out certain agreements. Blockchain is not the only way for agents to collaborate: traditional APIs, databases, and payment networks can also serve that purpose. But blockchain may offer distinctive benefits when collaboration crosses platforms, participants need shared verifiable state, or digital assets must be transferred.

AI lowers the barriers to creation; blockchain lowers the barriers to open collaboration. Together, they expand the space in which individuals can participate in economic activity.

6.Permissionlessness Does Not Eliminate Authority—It Redefines It#

Authority takes many forms. The professional standing earned by a scholar through decades of research is not the same as the power a platform holds because it controls access. The former arises from knowledge and judgment; the latter partly from control over resources and rules.

Permissionlessness does not mean rejecting expert authority. It means innovators should not have to seek approval merely because an institution controls the entry point. Science still needs experts to validate findings; startups still benefit from capital when scaling; commercial transactions still require law and mechanisms of real-world accountability. What changes is that these institutions need not always be prerequisites for beginning an experiment.

Authority will increasingly have to earn recognition by continuing to deliver value, rather than maintaining its position solely through control over access.

Discoveries may emerge from AI or cross-disciplinary explorers, while professional communities continue to play essential roles in interpretation, validation, and the accumulation of knowledge. The frontier will be shaped not only by boundaries drawn by established authorities, but by those who actually contribute new knowledge.

7.From Web3 to AI: Permissionlessness Is Undergoing a Major Expansion#

Looking back across technological history, a clear intellectual trajectory emerges. The early internet emphasized open access and the freedom to innovate. Web3 sought to extend open rules to the exchange of value and digital collaboration. AI is now extending that openness further into the production of knowledge and the creation of products.

The internet lowered the barriers to distributing information. Blockchain seeks to lower the barriers to exchanging value and collaborating openly. Artificial intelligence is lowering the barriers to producing knowledge and creating products.

None of these technologies is a perfect solution. AI may create new concentrations of model capabilities and compute; blockchain also faces governance challenges, costs, and centralization bottlenecks. Permissionlessness, therefore, is not a destination already reached but a direction worth pursuing.

When evaluating technological progress, we should ask not only how powerful new capabilities are, but also how widely they are available, who decides who can participate, and whether ordinary people can create value without depending on a few gatekeepers.

8.What Does This Mean for Young People Today?#

For university students, graduate researchers, and young people just entering the workforce, this transformation has immediate relevance: they are choosing the direction of the next several decades of their lives. Traditionally, attending a good university, choosing a promising major, pursuing research under an adviser, and joining a prestigious institution offered a relatively clear path. Those paths still have value, but some of the assumptions behind them are changing.

The Purpose of Learning Is Changing

AI can help explain theories, write programs, analyze data, and even support frontier research. Memorizing facts or mechanically completing prescribed tasks may no longer provide a durable advantage. This does not make foundational knowledge unimportant. On the contrary, a solid foundation enables us to judge whether AI-generated answers are trustworthy and to ask questions that matter.

The focus of learning must shift from mastering answers others already know to identifying important questions, exercising independent judgment, and creating new value.

Do Not Let Your Discipline Define Your Limits Too Early

A doctoral student may spend years investigating a particular problem. If AI rapidly advances that problem, what is the lasting asset: the solution to one question, or the ability to understand a discipline, recognize important problems, and keep exploring the unknown? A specific research topic may be resolved sooner than expected, but the ability to discover new questions, develop explanatory frameworks, and assess the value of research will not become obsolete.

Do not define your entire life by a single unsolved problem. Strive instead to become someone who can continually discover new questions.

Do Not Wait for Someone Else to Tell You That You Are Ready

If you have a startup idea, you need not wait until graduation, financing, or a complete team before building a prototype. If a problem in another discipline interests you, AI can help you read the literature and explore hypotheses before you seek out qualified experts to test them together. AI does not guarantee that every idea will succeed. But it makes it more realistic to act first and demonstrate capability through practice.

In the past, young people often had to prove they were capable before being given a chance to try. In the future, more of them will be able to prove their capability by trying.

The Future Belongs to Those Who Can Define Their Own Direction

Do not treat the judgments of schools, advisers, employers, or investors as the sole basis for deciding whether to act. Their views remain important, but they should not define the full boundaries of personal exploration. Permissionlessness offers young people no guarantee of success—only more room to try independently. Greater freedom also demands greater responsibility in judgment.

Stay curious. Think independently. Cross disciplinary boundaries, make good use of new technologies, and have the courage to put ideas into practice. As getting started becomes easier, perhaps the hardest question will be: what is worth committing to for the long term?

9.What Does This Mean for Public Policy Today?#

If permissionlessness is expanding young people’s opportunities to explore and create independently, public policy must confront another question: will those opportunities actually be accessible to people across differences in income, education, and geography? Lower technical barriers do not automatically guarantee equal participation. If only a few well-capitalized institutions can afford the most advanced models and computational resources, AI could widen disparities among families, schools, and businesses even as it raises overall productivity.

For AI, a central task of public policy is to make compute and advanced model capabilities broadly accessible. Governments and universities can provide shared computing resources, educational and research compute credits, open technical standards, and stronger market competition so students, independent researchers, and small startup teams can access the AI tools they need at affordable cost. The U.S. National Science Foundation’s National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource (NAIRR) pilot has explored providing compute, models, data, and support services to researchers and educators. Public investment, however, must account for costs, resource allocation, and safety boundaries; universal access does not mean unlimited free computing. [10]

For blockchain, the government’s role is more focused on establishing clear, predictable, risk-proportionate rules: distinguishing open-source developers who do not control customer funds from institutions that actually provide custody or financial intermediation; preserving room for lawful software development, protocol deployment, and participation in open networks while protecting consumers and combating fraud and money laundering; and reducing dependence on individual platforms through open standards, education, and interoperability. U.S. legislative efforts involving stablecoin regulation and the legal obligations of blockchain developers illustrate the need to balance these competing policy objectives. [11][12]

Universities, businesses, and nonprofit organizations also have roles to play: opening up cross-disciplinary opportunities, creating shared development environments, and providing security and compliance education so young people without substantial financial resources or industry connections can turn ideas into reality. Governments need not choose every person’s research direction or startup project, but they can work to remove unnecessary barriers to entry.

As technology increasingly makes creation possible without prior permission, institutions and public resources have a responsibility to prevent new concentrations of computing power and new regulatory barriers from making that freedom the privilege of a few.

True permissionlessness should mean more than “anyone is theoretically allowed to try.” It should help ensure that people from different backgrounds have a practical opportunity to explore. Open participation and necessary regulation are not mutually exclusive: the former broadens the possibilities for innovation, while the latter helps establish boundaries of accountability, trust, and fair competition.

Conclusion: Innovation Need Not Wait for Permission#

Returning to OpenAI’s mathematics announcement, perhaps the most thought-provoking question is not simply how many difficult problems AI has solved, but that a new capacity for exploring knowledge is emerging—and established institutions are still learning how to accommodate it. Similar changes may unfold in software, entrepreneurship, research, design, and financial services.

AI needs reliability, safety, and accountability; blockchain still faces challenges of scalability, user experience, real-world fulfillment, and regulation. Permissionlessness has never meant ignoring rules or other people’s rights. But the gateways to innovation are genuinely widening.

In the past, people who wanted to create something often asked first: Do I have enough money? Do I have a team? Have I earned an institution’s approval? Will someone give me a chance? Increasingly, they will be able to begin with a different question: What do I want to create?

The future of innovation should be defined less by who is deemed qualified to begin, and more by who actually creates value.

For the younger generation, this may be an especially promising time. They need not spend much of their lives seeking permission to enter an established system before finding their own opportunities to create. They can explore interests earlier, cross disciplinary boundaries more boldly, and turn ideas into reality more directly.

We are moving from an age in which a few decide who is qualified to create toward one in which more people can create independently and demonstrate their value through what they build.

You do not need to wait for the world to give you permission before you begin creating your own future.

核心观点

2026 年 10 月，OpenAI 公布由 AI 生成的数学研究成果并引发数学界的争议，让一个词重新进入人们的视野：Permissionless（无需许可）。区块链与 AI 正从两个方向推动同一场变革：区块链让互不相识的人依照公开规则交易与协作，而不必依赖中心机构；AI 让个人无需先获得资本、软件供应商或学术机构的认可，就能开发产品、构建工具，甚至参与科学研究。Permissionless 不是消灭专业权威和必要的监管，而是让权威必须通过持续贡献来赢得认可。对年轻人而言，这意味着不必等待别人告诉你已经准备好了；对公共政策而言，则需要让算力与开放网络普惠可及，避免这种自由沦为少数人的特权。

2026 年 10 月，在 All-In Podcast 对 OpenAI 数学研究成果及数学界反应的讨论中，一个词格外引人深思：Permissionless。直译过来，就是“不需要许可”。这个词并不新鲜。几年前 Web3 与区块链最火热时，Permissionless 几乎是整个行业的核心理念之一。但在 AI 时代，将它用于讨论数学研究乃至人类知识的发展，却让人产生一种新的认识。[1]

区块链与人工智能，可能正在从两个方向推动同一场变革：让越来越多原本需要权威批准、机构背书或大量资源才能完成的事情，不再需要事先获得许可。

一、当 AI 开始挑战数学界的权威#

2026 年 10 月 6 日，OpenAI 公布了一批由其内部前沿 AI 模型生成的数学研究成果，涉及数学及理论计算机科学中的大量开放问题。根据当时公布的资料，模型尝试了约 4,000 个问题，整理出 372 组研究成果，最初发布 722 份研究文稿，其中包括对部分长期开放问题的探索与推进。尤其引人注目的是 OpenAI 披露的计算成本：平均每项成果消耗的计算资源，相当于 ChatGPT Pro 约三个小时的思考计算量。这里的“三小时”指等效计算量，而非解决一道难题的实际经过时间；这些文稿也不能全部视为已通过独立验证的完整证明，部分结论仍需要进一步审查与确认。[2][3]

即便如此，这件事仍然意义重大。数学是人类最古老、最严谨的知识领域之一。有些问题历经数代研究仍未得到满意解答；一位博士生可能为某个问题投入多年，一位教授甚至可能把整个职业生涯建立在同一研究方向上。如今，AI 开始参与这些领域的证明探索、方法发现与研究推进。

过去，数学前沿主要由接受专业训练的研究者推进。学术机构、研究基金、同行评审和专业共同体，构成了知识生产的重要制度体系。这些机制具有无法替代的价值；但现在，并不具有博士学位或学术职位的 AI 系统，也能直接参与最前沿的问题探索。

数学界的反应

OpenAI 的发布引起数学界争论。Association for Human Mathematics（AHM）发表声明，批评相关发布方式，并对学术规范、研究质量、人类理解以及科研职业生态表达担忧。陶哲轩（Terence Tao）在博客上转载了相关声明；转载不意味着他赞同其中全部观点。[4]

数学界的反应，不能简单理解为维护既得利益。数学证明需要严格验证，学术成果需要明确归属，人类还需要理解答案为什么成立。这些是真实而重要的关切。

但争论揭示的另一面同样无法回避：当一种新技术具备了独立推进知识前沿的能力，传统知识共同体便不再独占发现的过程。数学家仍可以质疑证明、要求验证、讨论如何将成果纳入人类知识体系，却难以仅凭拒绝参与便阻止 AI 继续探索。

谁来定义知识的前沿？

这场变革的意义，可能还不止于知识生产方式的改变。它正在挑战一个长期被视为理所当然的假设：一个领域的前沿，主要由这个领域的权威来定义。过去，什么问题重要、哪些方向值得投入资源、什么成果算作突破，往往由顶尖专家、研究机构和专业共同体形成判断。

AI 使这一格局发生变化。未来推动一个学科前沿的，可能不仅是该学科中最资深的专家，也可能是来自其他领域的研究者与 AI 的共同探索。物理学家可能借助 AI 提出数学思路；软件工程师可能在生命科学中发现值得检验的新假设；一个没有经过传统专业训练、却具有敏锐问题意识的人，也可能发现过去被忽略的问题。

这并不等于专业知识失去价值。验证、解释与建立理论体系依然需要深厚能力。但专业能力和定义前沿的独占权不是一回事。

过去，权威在很大程度上定义前沿；未来，前沿将越来越多地由不同背景的人与 AI 共同探索，而权威需要通过对新前沿的理解、验证与贡献，持续证明自身的价值。

Permissionless 不只是让更多人拥有参与创新的资格，它还让更多人获得参与定义未来的机会。

二、Permissionless 并不是 Web3 发明的#

Permissionless 的理念远早于区块链。早期互联网的发展带有鲜明的开放创新精神：任何人都可以开发网站、发布服务、尝试新的网络应用，而不必先取得某个统一管理者的批准。这种开放性，是互联网创新活力的重要来源。

2014 年，技术政策研究者 Adam Thierer 出版《Permissionless Innovation: The Continuing Case for Comprehensive Technological Freedom》，系统讨论“无需事前许可即可开展技术创新”的原则。他也曾说明，自己不是这一术语的最早创造者。[5]

创新应该以自由尝试为起点，而不是以事先获得许可为前提。

这是一种逐渐形成的工程文化与制度理念；区块链则使其中的一部分原则获得了更具体的技术实现。

三、Web3：让交易与协作不再依赖中心机构#

2009 年，比特币网络开始运行，提出了与传统金融基础设施不同的组织方式。过去，两个人进行远程价值转移，通常需要银行、支付网络或其他可信机构来维护账本与执行交易。比特币则试图通过密码学、分布式账本和共识机制，让互不认识的参与者按公开规则转移价值。[6]

以太坊随后将这一思想扩展到智能合约。对于开放的公有区块链而言，参与、验证和使用协议，原则上不需要中心机构按身份逐一审批。[7]

从信任机构到信任规则

传统商业合作常依赖银行、平台、律师或担保方作为中介。智能合约可以把部分约定写成可公开检查、按条件执行的程序，例如锁定交易资金并在约定条件满足时支付。它不能消灭代码漏洞、现实履约、链外数据真伪等问题，但可以减少某些场景对单一中介的依赖。

过去，我们需要信任某个机构会按照约定执行交易；现在，在一些场景中，我们可以依赖公开且可验证的程序规则。

从平台控制到可转移的数字权利

设想一家酒店销售高价值的预付住宿套餐。传统模式下，转让常须经酒店或预订平台确认。酒店也可以选择把符合条件的套餐权益与 NFT 关联，在合同及系统中认可链上持有人记录。这样，在满足相关条款、法律和核销要求时，后续交易便可能无需酒店逐笔人工批准。

但 NFT 本身不会自动产生现实世界的住宿权。真正使方案成立的，是酒店的履约承诺、法律安排，以及链上可验证的凭证与记录。Permissionless 并非取消现实义务，而是在约定基础上开放转让方式。

四、AI：让创造本身变得 Permissionless#

如果说区块链改变的是人与人之间协作、交易和建立信任的方式，那么 AI 正在改变人类创造知识、产品和服务的能力。

创业不再必须先获得资本的认可

过去，即使一个人有出色的商业想法，也可能必须先寻找风险投资，才能聘请工程师、设计师与产品经理，将概念变成产品。在这样的条件下，资本事实上承担了某种守门人的角色。

AI 正在降低产品开发的初始门槛。今天，创业者能够借助编程助手与智能体做出原型、编写前后端代码、执行部分测试和运营流程。复杂系统当然仍需专业工程、安全审查和持续维护，但创业者越来越可以先创造产品、验证市场，再决定是否融资。

过去，创业者往往需要先说服资本，才能获得创造产品的能力；现在，创业者越来越可能先创造产品，再用产品证明自己的价值。

VC 不会因此消失。资本仍能帮助企业扩张、拓展渠道和承担重大风险；它只是可能从某些项目的必要前提，变成可选择的增长工具。

企业不再必须依赖大型软件供应商

过去，企业要改进内部流程，往往需要购买企业软件或支付高额定制开发费用。对许多中小企业而言，不少问题因成本太高而长期没有得到解决。现在，它们有机会借助 AI 创建更贴合自身订单、库存、客服或数据分析流程的小型软件。

关键业务仍然需要安全、隐私、合规与维护保障，但方向已经很清楚：从购买别人设计好的解决方案，走向根据自身需求直接创造解决方案。过去业务流程常常不得不适应软件，未来软件将越来越能够适应业务。

科学研究不再只属于少数专业机构

前沿研究依然需要深厚专业知识、实验条件和严谨验证。但 AI 能够协助阅读文献、发现潜在联系、提出假设、编写模拟程序，从而让更多背景不同的人参与研究探索。一个人的研究潜力，将不再完全由其所在机构、团队规模和可以调动的资源决定。

Permissionless 改变的是参与资格，而不是正确性的标准。

五、两种看似矛盾的技术，为什么正在走向统一？#

Peter Thiel 曾在与 Reid Hoffman 的公开对谈中，以加密技术偏重自由主义、AI 容易走向集中控制的对照，描述两种技术在权力结构上的张力。区块链追求开放参与和减少中介，而先进 AI 模型的训练依赖巨大的算力、数据与资本，因此可能集中在少数企业手中。[8]

但若将目光从基础设施转向使用者，情况就变得不同：训练能力可能集中，而应用层的创造能力却可能广泛扩散。普通开发者不需要建设数据中心，就可以借助模型编写软件；小团队无需训练基础模型，也可以构建产品。

AI 提供创造能力，区块链提供开放协作的基础设施

风险投资机构 Andreessen Horowitz（a16z）联合创始人 Ben Horowitz 在 2025 年 11 月与 Costis Maglaras 的访谈中，从计算技术史提出一个观察：每一代计算范式的发展，都离不开计算机与网络的相互促进。从大型机与企业网络、个人电脑与局域网，到互联网与智能手机，二者技术基础虽然不同，结合后却能释放单独使用时难以实现的价值。由此他提出一个面向 AI 时代的问题：智能体需要什么样的网络？如果 AI Agent 不再只是回答问题，而是能够在用户授权下购买服务、收取款项和参与经济活动，那么面向人类账户设计的传统支付机制并不总能直接适用。他认为加密货币可能成为智能体使用的互联网原生价值交换工具；密码学技术还可用于验证内容来源与身份、减少深度伪造带来的信任问题，并借助公钥体系和零知识证明，使交易参与者在不交出全部敏感数据的情况下证明特定资格。这些技术尚未成为普遍实现的行业现实，但已经揭示出 AI 与区块链在未来经济结构中的互补关系：AI 扩展“我们能创造什么”，区块链探索“我们如何在开放规则下协作、交易与分配价值”。前者提供新的智能与创造能力，后者则有望为跨主体的支付、数字权属及可验证协作提供开放基础设施。[9]

例如，不同公司的 AI Agent 可以通过开放协议发现服务、交换信息、完成付款，并使用智能合约执行某些约定。区块链不是智能体协作的唯一方式：传统 API、数据库及支付网络同样可行。但在跨平台协作、共享可验证状态和转移数字资产时，它可能提供独特价值。

AI 降低创造门槛，区块链降低开放协作门槛。两者从不同方向扩大个人参与经济活动的空间。

六、Permissionless 不是消灭权威，而是重新定义权威#

权威并不只有一种。学者凭借长期研究获得的专业声望，与某个平台因为掌握入口而拥有的审批权，性质并不相同。前者来自知识与判断力，后者则部分来自对资源和规则的控制。

Permissionless 的意义，不是否定专业权威，而是让创新者不必仅仅因为某个机构控制入口，就必须先征得其认可。科学领域仍需要专家验证；创业仍需要资本帮助扩张；商业交易仍需要法律与现实世界的责任机制。改变的是，它们不再必然是任何尝试得以开始的先决条件。

权威将越来越需要通过持续提供价值来获得认可，而不能仅仅依靠控制入口来维持地位。

发现可以来自 AI 或跨领域探索者，专业共同体则在解释、验证和积累知识上继续发挥关键作用。前沿的形成不再只取决于既有权威如何划定边界，也取决于谁能真正推动新知识产生。

七、从 Web3 到 AI：Permissionless 正在完成一次重要扩展#

回望技术发展，可以看到一条清晰的思想脉络：早期互联网强调开放接入与自由创新；Web3 尝试将开放规则延伸到价值交换和数字协作；AI 则正在把这种开放性进一步扩展到知识生产与产品创造。

互联网降低了信息传播的门槛。区块链试图降低价值交换和开放协作的门槛。人工智能正在降低知识生产和产品创造的门槛。

这些技术都不是完美答案。AI 仍可能形成新的模型与算力集中，区块链也存在治理、成本与中心化瓶颈。Permissionless 因此不是一个已经实现的终点，而是一种值得追求的方向。

衡量技术进步时，我们不仅要问新能力有多强，还要问这些能力向多少人开放、谁决定谁可以参与，以及普通人能否不依赖少数守门人来创造价值。

八、对于今天的年轻人，这意味着什么？#

对于正在读大学、攻读研究生学位或刚踏入社会的年轻人，这场变化具有直接的意义，因为他们正在决定自己未来几十年的方向。过去，进入好大学、选择有前景的专业、在导师指导下研究、加入知名机构，是相对清晰的道路。这些路径仍有价值，但其背后的某些假设正在变化。

学习的目的，正在发生变化

AI 可以帮助解释理论、编写程序、分析数据甚至辅助前沿研究。单纯记忆知识或机械完成既定任务，可能不再足以构成长期优势。这并不是说基础知识不重要；恰恰相反，扎实的知识基础让我们能够判断 AI 的答案是否可信，也使我们有能力提出有意义的问题。

学习的重心，需要从掌握别人已经知道的答案，转向发现重要问题、独立判断和创造新价值。

不要过早让专业定义自己的边界

一个博士生可能投入数年研究某个具体问题。如果 AI 能快速推进这一问题，那么值得思考的长期资产是什么：是一道题目的解答，还是理解学科、识别重要问题、不断探索未知的能力？具体课题可能被提前解决，但发现新问题、建立解释框架和判断研究价值的能力不会因此过时。

不要将自己的一生定义为某一道尚未解决的题目，而应该努力成为一个能够不断发现新问题的人。

不要等待别人告诉你，你已经准备好了

有创业想法，不一定要等到毕业、融资或组建完整团队才做原型；对另一学科的问题感兴趣，也可以借助 AI 阅读文献、探索假设，再寻找合适的专业人士共同验证。AI 不会让所有想法成功，但它让先行动、再通过实践证明能力成为更现实的选择。

过去，年轻人往往需要先证明自己有能力，才能获得尝试的机会；未来，越来越多的年轻人可以通过不断尝试，来证明自己的能力。

未来属于能够自主定义方向的人

不要将学校、导师、企业或投资人的评价当作自己行动的唯一依据。它们仍然重要，但不该成为个人探索的全部边界。Permissionless 给予年轻人的不是成功保证，而是更多自主尝试的空间。更大的自由也意味着更大的判断责任。

保持好奇，独立思考，跨越学科边界，善用新技术，并勇于将想法付诸实践。随着“开始”变得越来越容易，真正困难的问题或许将是：什么值得长期投入？

九、对于今天的公共政策，这意味着什么？#

如果说 Permissionless 正在扩大年轻人自主探索和创造的机会，那么今天的公共政策需要回答另一个问题：这些机会能否真正被不同收入、不同教育和不同地域背景的人获得？技术上降低门槛，并不自动意味着社会中的每个人都能够平等参与。如果最先进的模型与算力只能由少数资本雄厚的机构负担，AI 可能在提升整体生产力的同时，进一步扩大不同家庭、学校与企业之间的能力差距。

从 AI 的角度，公共政策的一项重要任务，是推动算力与先进模型能力的普惠可及。政府和高校可以通过共享计算资源、教育与科研算力额度、开放技术标准和促进市场竞争，让学生、独立研究者及小型创业团队以可承受的成本使用必要的 AI 工具。美国国家科学基金会（NSF）的 National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource（NAIRR）试点，已经探索向研究人员及教育群体提供算力、模型、数据和支持服务。但公共投入仍需考虑成本、资源分配与安全边界，并不意味着无限制地免费提供算力。[10]

从区块链的角度，政府的作用则更偏向于建立清晰、可预期且与风险相称的制度环境：区分不控制用户资金的开源开发者与实际经营托管或金融中介业务的机构；在保障消费者、打击诈骗与洗钱的同时，为合法的软件开发、协议部署和开放网络参与保留空间；通过开放标准、教育和互操作性减少对单一平台的依赖。美国围绕稳定币监管与区块链开发者责任边界的立法实践，正体现出这些不同政策目标之间需要权衡。[11][12]

大学、企业和公益组织也能够参与：开放跨学科实践机会，建设共享开发环境，提供安全与合规教育，帮助缺乏资金和行业关系的年轻人将想法付诸实践。政府不必替每个人选择研究方向或创业项目，却可以努力降低不必要的进入壁垒。

技术让创造越来越不需要事先许可；制度与公共资源的责任，是避免新的算力集中和规则壁垒，使这种自由不至于只属于少数人。

真正的 Permissionless，不应只是“理论上允许任何人尝试”，还应尽可能使不同背景的人有现实的条件参与探索。开放参与与必要监管并不相互排斥：前者扩大创新的可能，后者帮助确立责任、信任与公平竞争的边界。

结语：创新不必先得到许可#

回到 OpenAI 的数学事件，最值得深思的，或许并不只是 AI 究竟解决了多少数学难题，而是一种新的知识探索能力正在出现，传统体系尚在学习如何接纳它。类似的变化可能陆续发生于软件、创业、科研、设计和金融服务。

AI 需要可靠性、安全性和责任机制；区块链需要继续解决扩展性、体验、履约与监管问题。Permissionless 从来不意味着无视规则与他人的权利。但创新的入口确实正在扩大。

过去，人们想创造某种东西，常常先问：我有没有足够的钱？有没有团队？有没有得到机构认可？有没有人愿意给我机会？未来，越来越多人将有机会先问：我究竟想创造什么？

未来的创新，不应仅由谁有资格开始来定义，而应越来越由谁真正创造了价值来决定。

对年轻一代而言，这也许是一个特别值得期待的时代。他们不必把人生的大部分时间用于争取进入某个体系的资格，才开始寻找属于自己的创造机会。他们可以更早探索兴趣，更大胆跨越学科边界，更直接地把想法变成现实。

我们正在从一个由少数人决定谁有资格创造的时代，走向一个让更多人能够自主创造、并以创造本身证明价值的时代。

你不需要等待世界给你许可，才开始创造属于自己的未来。

核心觀點

2026 年 10 月，OpenAI 公佈由 AI 生成的數學研究成果並引發數學界的爭議，讓一個詞重新進入人們的視野：Permissionless（無需許可）。區塊鏈與 AI 正從兩個方向推動同一場變革：區塊鏈讓互不相識的人依照公開規則交易與協作，而不必依賴中心機構；AI 讓個人無需先獲得資本、軟件供應商或學術機構的認可，就能開發產品、構建工具，甚至參與科學研究。Permissionless 不是消滅專業權威和必要的監管，而是讓權威必須通過持續貢獻來贏得認可。對年輕人而言，這意味著不必等待別人告訴你已經準備好了；對公共政策而言，則需要讓算力與開放網絡普惠可及，避免這種自由淪為少數人的特權。

2026 年 10 月，在 All-In Podcast 對 OpenAI 數學研究成果及數學界反應的討論中，一個詞格外引人深思：Permissionless。直譯過來，就是“不需要許可”。這個詞並不新鮮。幾年前 Web3 與區塊鏈最火熱時，Permissionless 幾乎是整個行業的核心理念之一。但在 AI 時代，將它用於討論數學研究乃至人類知識的發展，卻讓人產生一種新的認識。[1]

區塊鏈與人工智能，可能正在從兩個方向推動同一場變革：讓越來越多原本需要權威批准、機構背書或大量資源才能完成的事情，不再需要事先獲得許可。

一、當 AI 開始挑戰數學界的權威#

2026 年 10 月 6 日，OpenAI 公佈了一批由其內部前沿 AI 模型生成的數學研究成果，涉及數學及理論計算機科學中的大量開放問題。根據當時公佈的資料，模型嘗試了約 4,000 個問題，整理出 372 組研究成果，最初發布 722 份研究文稿，其中包括對部分長期開放問題的探索與推進。尤其引人注目的是 OpenAI 披露的計算成本：平均每項成果消耗的計算資源，相當於 ChatGPT Pro 約三個小時的思考計算量。這裡的“三小時”指等效計算量，而非解決一道難題的實際經過時間；這些文稿也不能全部視為已通過獨立驗證的完整證明，部分結論仍需要進一步審查與確認。[2][3]

即便如此，這件事仍然意義重大。數學是人類最古老、最嚴謹的知識領域之一。有些問題歷經數代研究仍未得到滿意解答；一位博士生可能為某個問題投入多年，一位教授甚至可能把整個職業生涯建立在同一研究方向上。如今，AI 開始參與這些領域的證明探索、方法發現與研究推進。

過去，數學前沿主要由接受專業訓練的研究者推進。學術機構、研究基金、同行評審和專業共同體，構成了知識生產的重要制度體系。這些機制具有無法替代的價值；但現在，並不具有博士學位或學術職位的 AI 系統，也能直接參與最前沿的問題探索。

數學界的反應

OpenAI 的發佈引起數學界爭論。Association for Human Mathematics（AHM）發表聲明，批評相關發佈方式，並對學術規範、研究質量、人類理解以及科研職業生態表達擔憂。陶哲軒（Terence Tao）在博客上轉載了相關聲明；轉載不意味著他贊同其中全部觀點。[4]

數學界的反應，不能簡單理解為維護既得利益。數學證明需要嚴格驗證，學術成果需要明確歸屬，人類還需要理解答案為什麼成立。這些是真實而重要的關切。

但爭論揭示的另一面同樣無法迴避：當一種新技術具備了獨立推進知識前沿的能力，傳統知識共同體便不再獨佔發現的過程。數學家仍可以質疑證明、要求驗證、討論如何將成果納入人類知識體系，卻難以僅憑拒絕參與便阻止 AI 繼續探索。

誰來定義知識的前沿？

這場變革的意義，可能還不止於知識生產方式的改變。它正在挑戰一個長期被視為理所當然的假設：一個領域的前沿，主要由這個領域的權威來定義。過去，什麼問題重要、哪些方向值得投入資源、什麼成果算作突破，往往由頂尖專家、研究機構和專業共同體形成判斷。

AI 使這一格局發生變化。未來推動一個學科前沿的，可能不僅是該學科中最資深的專家，也可能是來自其他領域的研究者與 AI 的共同探索。物理學家可能借助 AI 提出數學思路；軟件工程師可能在生命科學中發現值得檢驗的新假設；一個沒有經過傳統專業訓練、卻具有敏銳問題意識的人，也可能發現過去被忽略的問題。

這並不等於專業知識失去價值。驗證、解釋與建立理論體系依然需要深厚能力。但專業能力和定義前沿的獨佔權不是一回事。

過去，權威在很大程度上定義前沿；未來，前沿將越來越多地由不同背景的人與 AI 共同探索，而權威需要通過對新前沿的理解、驗證與貢獻，持續證明自身的價值。

Permissionless 不只是讓更多人擁有參與創新的資格，它還讓更多人獲得參與定義未來的機會。

二、Permissionless 並不是 Web3 發明的#

Permissionless 的理念遠早於區塊鏈。早期互聯網的發展帶有鮮明的開放創新精神：任何人都可以開發網站、發佈服務、嘗試新的網絡應用，而不必先取得某個統一管理者的批准。這種開放性，是互聯網創新活力的重要來源。

2014 年，技術政策研究者 Adam Thierer 出版《Permissionless Innovation: The Continuing Case for Comprehensive Technological Freedom》，系統討論“無需事前許可即可開展技術創新”的原則。他也曾說明，自己不是這一術語的最早創造者。[5]

創新應該以自由嘗試為起點，而不是以事先獲得許可為前提。

這是一種逐漸形成的工程文化與制度理念；區塊鏈則使其中的一部分原則獲得了更具體的技術實現。

三、Web3：讓交易與協作不再依賴中心機構#

2009 年，比特幣網絡開始運行，提出了與傳統金融基礎設施不同的組織方式。過去，兩個人進行遠程價值轉移，通常需要銀行、支付網絡或其他可信機構來維護賬本與執行交易。比特幣則試圖通過密碼學、分佈式賬本和共識機制，讓互不認識的參與者按公開規則轉移價值。[6]

以太坊隨後將這一思想擴展到智能合約。對於開放的公有區塊鏈而言，參與、驗證和使用協議，原則上不需要中心機構按身份逐一審批。[7]

從信任機構到信任規則

傳統商業合作常依賴銀行、平臺、律師或擔保方作為中介。智能合約可以把部分約定寫成可公開檢查、按條件執行的程序，例如鎖定交易資金並在約定條件滿足時支付。它不能消滅代碼漏洞、現實履約、鏈外數據真偽等問題，但可以減少某些場景對單一中介的依賴。

過去，我們需要信任某個機構會按照約定執行交易；現在，在一些場景中，我們可以依賴公開且可驗證的程序規則。

從平臺控制到可轉移的數字權利

設想一家酒店銷售高價值的預付住宿套餐。傳統模式下，轉讓常須經酒店或預訂平臺確認。酒店也可以選擇把符合條件的套餐權益與 NFT 關聯，在合同及系統中認可鏈上持有人記錄。這樣，在滿足相關條款、法律和核銷要求時，後續交易便可能無需酒店逐筆人工批准。

但 NFT 本身不會自動產生現實世界的住宿權。真正使方案成立的，是酒店的履約承諾、法律安排，以及鏈上可驗證的憑證與記錄。Permissionless 並非取消現實義務，而是在約定基礎上開放轉讓方式。

四、AI：讓創造本身變得 Permissionless#

如果說區塊鏈改變的是人與人之間協作、交易和建立信任的方式，那麼 AI 正在改變人類創造知識、產品和服務的能力。

創業不再必須先獲得資本的認可

過去，即使一個人有出色的商業想法，也可能必須先尋找風險投資，才能聘請工程師、設計師與產品經理，將概念變成產品。在這樣的條件下，資本事實上承擔了某種守門人的角色。

AI 正在降低產品開發的初始門檻。今天，創業者能夠藉助編程助手與智能體做出原型、編寫前後端代碼、執行部分測試和運營流程。複雜系統當然仍需專業工程、安全審查和持續維護，但創業者越來越可以先創造產品、驗證市場，再決定是否融資。

過去，創業者往往需要先說服資本，才能獲得創造產品的能力；現在，創業者越來越可能先創造產品，再用產品證明自己的價值。

VC 不會因此消失。資本仍能幫助企業擴張、拓展渠道和承擔重大風險；它只是可能從某些項目的必要前提，變成可選擇的增長工具。

企業不再必須依賴大型軟件供應商

過去，企業要改進內部流程，往往需要購買企業軟件或支付高額定製開發費用。對許多中小企業而言，不少問題因成本太高而長期沒有得到解決。現在，它們有機會藉助 AI 創建更貼合自身訂單、庫存、客服或數據分析流程的小型軟件。

關鍵業務仍然需要安全、隱私、合規與維護保障，但方向已經很清楚：從購買別人設計好的解決方案，走向根據自身需求直接創造解決方案。過去業務流程常常不得不適應軟件，未來軟件將越來越能夠適應業務。

科學研究不再只屬於少數專業機構

前沿研究依然需要深厚專業知識、實驗條件和嚴謹驗證。但 AI 能夠協助閱讀文獻、發現潛在聯繫、提出假設、編寫模擬程序，從而讓更多背景不同的人參與研究探索。一個人的研究潛力，將不再完全由其所在機構、團隊規模和可以調動的資源決定。

Permissionless 改變的是參與資格，而不是正確性的標準。

五、兩種看似矛盾的技術，為什麼正在走向統一？#

Peter Thiel 曾在與 Reid Hoffman 的公開對談中，以加密技術偏重自由主義、AI 容易走向集中控制的對照，描述兩種技術在權力結構上的張力。區塊鏈追求開放參與和減少中介，而先進 AI 模型的訓練依賴巨大的算力、數據與資本，因此可能集中在少數企業手中。[8]

但若將目光從基礎設施轉向使用者，情況就變得不同：訓練能力可能集中，而應用層的創造能力卻可能廣泛擴散。普通開發者不需要建設數據中心，就可以藉助模型編寫軟件；小團隊無需訓練基礎模型，也可以構建產品。

AI 提供創造能力，區塊鏈提供開放協作的基礎設施

風險投資機構 Andreessen Horowitz（a16z）聯合創始人 Ben Horowitz 在 2025 年 11 月與 Costis Maglaras 的訪談中，從計算技術史提出一個觀察：每一代計算範式的發展，都離不開計算機與網絡的相互促進。從大型機與企業網絡、個人電腦與局域網，到互聯網與智能手機，二者技術基礎雖然不同，結合後卻能釋放單獨使用時難以實現的價值。由此他提出一個面向 AI 時代的問題：智能體需要什麼樣的網絡？如果 AI Agent 不再只是回答問題，而是能夠在用戶授權下購買服務、收取款項和參與經濟活動，那麼面向人類賬戶設計的傳統支付機制並不總能直接適用。他認為加密貨幣可能成為智能體使用的互聯網原生價值交換工具；密碼學技術還可用於驗證內容來源與身份、減少深度偽造帶來的信任問題，並藉助公鑰體系和零知識證明，使交易參與者在不交出全部敏感數據的情況下證明特定資格。這些技術尚未成為普遍實現的行業現實，但已經揭示出 AI 與區塊鏈在未來經濟結構中的互補關係：AI 擴展“我們能創造什麼”，區塊鏈探索“我們如何在開放規則下協作、交易與分配價值”。前者提供新的智能與創造能力，後者則有望為跨主體的支付、數字權屬及可驗證協作提供開放基礎設施。[9]

例如，不同公司的 AI Agent 可以通過開放協議發現服務、交換信息、完成付款，並使用智能合約執行某些約定。區塊鏈不是智能體協作的唯一方式：傳統 API、數據庫及支付網絡同樣可行。但在跨平臺協作、共享可驗證狀態和轉移數字資產時，它可能提供獨特價值。

AI 降低創造門檻，區塊鏈降低開放協作門檻。兩者從不同方向擴大個人參與經濟活動的空間。

六、Permissionless 不是消滅權威，而是重新定義權威#

權威並不只有一種。學者憑藉長期研究獲得的專業聲望，與某個平臺因為掌握入口而擁有的審批權，性質並不相同。前者來自知識與判斷力，後者則部分來自對資源和規則的控制。

Permissionless 的意義，不是否定專業權威，而是讓創新者不必僅僅因為某個機構控制入口，就必須先徵得其認可。科學領域仍需要專家驗證；創業仍需要資本幫助擴張；商業交易仍需要法律與現實世界的責任機制。改變的是，它們不再必然是任何嘗試得以開始的先決條件。

權威將越來越需要通過持續提供價值來獲得認可，而不能僅僅依靠控制入口來維持地位。

發現可以來自 AI 或跨領域探索者，專業共同體則在解釋、驗證和積累知識上繼續發揮關鍵作用。前沿的形成不再只取決於既有權威如何劃定邊界，也取決於誰能真正推動新知識產生。

七、從 Web3 到 AI：Permissionless 正在完成一次重要擴展#

回望技術發展，可以看到一條清晰的思想脈絡：早期互聯網強調開放接入與自由創新；Web3 嘗試將開放規則延伸到價值交換和數字協作；AI 則正在把這種開放性進一步擴展到知識生產與產品創造。

互聯網降低了信息傳播的門檻。區塊鏈試圖降低價值交換和開放協作的門檻。人工智能正在降低知識生產和產品創造的門檻。

這些技術都不是完美答案。AI 仍可能形成新的模型與算力集中，區塊鏈也存在治理、成本與中心化瓶頸。Permissionless 因此不是一個已經實現的終點，而是一種值得追求的方向。

衡量技術進步時，我們不僅要問新能力有多強，還要問這些能力向多少人開放、誰決定誰可以參與，以及普通人能否不依賴少數守門人來創造價值。

八、對於今天的年輕人，這意味著什麼？#

對於正在讀大學、攻讀研究生學位或剛踏入社會的年輕人，這場變化具有直接的意義，因為他們正在決定自己未來幾十年的方向。過去，進入好大學、選擇有前景的專業、在導師指導下研究、加入知名機構，是相對清晰的道路。這些路徑仍有價值，但其背後的某些假設正在變化。

學習的目的，正在發生變化

AI 可以幫助解釋理論、編寫程序、分析數據甚至輔助前沿研究。單純記憶知識或機械完成既定任務，可能不再足以構成長期優勢。這並不是說基礎知識不重要；恰恰相反，紮實的知識基礎讓我們能夠判斷 AI 的答案是否可信，也使我們有能力提出有意義的問題。

學習的重心，需要從掌握別人已經知道的答案，轉向發現重要問題、獨立判斷和創造新價值。

不要過早讓專業定義自己的邊界

一個博士生可能投入數年研究某個具體問題。如果 AI 能快速推進這一問題，那麼值得思考的長期資產是什麼：是一道題目的解答，還是理解學科、識別重要問題、不斷探索未知的能力？具體課題可能被提前解決，但發現新問題、建立解釋框架和判斷研究價值的能力不會因此過時。

不要將自己的一生定義為某一道尚未解決的題目，而應該努力成為一個能夠不斷髮現新問題的人。

不要等待別人告訴你，你已經準備好了

有創業想法，不一定要等到畢業、融資或組建完整團隊才做原型；對另一學科的問題感興趣，也可以藉助 AI 閱讀文獻、探索假設，再尋找合適的專業人士共同驗證。AI 不會讓所有想法成功，但它讓先行動、再通過實踐證明能力成為更現實的選擇。

過去，年輕人往往需要先證明自己有能力，才能獲得嘗試的機會；未來，越來越多的年輕人可以通過不斷嘗試，來證明自己的能力。

未來屬於能夠自主定義方向的人

不要將學校、導師、企業或投資人的評價當作自己行動的唯一依據。它們仍然重要，但不該成為個人探索的全部邊界。Permissionless 給予年輕人的不是成功保證，而是更多自主嘗試的空間。更大的自由也意味著更大的判斷責任。

保持好奇，獨立思考，跨越學科邊界，善用新技術，並勇於將想法付諸實踐。隨著“開始”變得越來越容易，真正困難的問題或許將是：什麼值得長期投入？

九、對於今天的公共政策，這意味著什麼？#

如果說 Permissionless 正在擴大年輕人自主探索和創造的機會，那麼今天的公共政策需要回答另一個問題：這些機會能否真正被不同收入、不同教育和不同地域背景的人獲得？技術上降低門檻，並不自動意味著社會中的每個人都能夠平等參與。如果最先進的模型與算力只能由少數資本雄厚的機構負擔，AI 可能在提升整體生產力的同時，進一步擴大不同家庭、學校與企業之間的能力差距。

從 AI 的角度，公共政策的一項重要任務，是推動算力與先進模型能力的普惠可及。政府和高校可以通過共享計算資源、教育與科研算力額度、開放技術標準和促進市場競爭，讓學生、獨立研究者及小型創業團隊以可承受的成本使用必要的 AI 工具。美國國家科學基金會（NSF）的 National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource（NAIRR）試點，已經探索向研究人員及教育群體提供算力、模型、數據和支持服務。但公共投入仍需考慮成本、資源分配與安全邊界，並不意味著無限制地免費提供算力。[10]

從區塊鏈的角度，政府的作用則更偏向於建立清晰、可預期且與風險相稱的制度環境：區分不控制用戶資金的開源開發者與實際經營託管或金融中介業務的機構；在保障消費者、打擊詐騙與洗錢的同時，為合法的軟件開發、協議部署和開放網絡參與保留空間；通過開放標準、教育和互操作性減少對單一平臺的依賴。美國圍繞穩定幣監管與區塊鏈開發者責任邊界的立法實踐，正體現出這些不同政策目標之間需要權衡。[11][12]

大學、企業和公益組織也能夠參與：開放跨學科實踐機會，建設共享開發環境，提供安全與合規教育，幫助缺乏資金和行業關係的年輕人將想法付諸實踐。政府不必替每個人選擇研究方向或創業項目，卻可以努力降低不必要的進入壁壘。

技術讓創造越來越不需要事先許可；制度與公共資源的責任，是避免新的算力集中和規則壁壘，使這種自由不至於只屬於少數人。

真正的 Permissionless，不應只是“理論上允許任何人嘗試”，還應儘可能使不同背景的人有現實的條件參與探索。開放參與與必要監管並不相互排斥：前者擴大創新的可能，後者幫助確立責任、信任與公平競爭的邊界。

結語：創新不必先得到許可#

回到 OpenAI 的數學事件，最值得深思的，或許並不只是 AI 究竟解決了多少數學難題，而是一種新的知識探索能力正在出現，傳統體系尚在學習如何接納它。類似的變化可能陸續發生於軟件、創業、科研、設計和金融服務。

AI 需要可靠性、安全性和責任機制；區塊鏈需要繼續解決擴展性、體驗、履約與監管問題。Permissionless 從來不意味著無視規則與他人的權利。但創新的入口確實正在擴大。

過去，人們想創造某種東西，常常先問：我有沒有足夠的錢？有沒有團隊？有沒有得到機構認可？有沒有人願意給我機會？未來，越來越多人將有機會先問：我究竟想創造什麼？

未來的創新，不應僅由誰有資格開始來定義，而應越來越由誰真正創造了價值來決定。

對年輕一代而言，這也許是一個特別值得期待的時代。他們不必把人生的大部分時間用於爭取進入某個體系的資格，才開始尋找屬於自己的創造機會。他們可以更早探索興趣，更大膽跨越學科邊界，更直接地把想法變成現實。

我們正在從一個由少數人決定誰有資格創造的時代，走向一個讓更多人能夠自主創造、並以創造本身證明價值的時代。

你不需要等待世界給你許可，才開始創造屬於自己的未來。
