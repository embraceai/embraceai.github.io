Our Intellect and Imagination我们的理性与想象我們的理性與想象

By Mark Li李曦李曦Aug 3, 20252025年8月3日2025年8月3日2 min read2 分钟阅读2 分鐘閱讀
TL;DR

Starting from Naval Ravikant's post that physics is the least compressible intellectual domain and poetry the least compressible artistic one, the essay notes how striking this is when LLMs compress vast human knowledge into a few terabytes. Physics resists compression through nature's irreducible complexity, though its equations are themselves extreme compressions. Poetry resists through irreducible depth: a summary captures only one reading and loses its ambiguity, music and emotion. Both remind us that some core always escapes simplification, challenging our intellect and imagination.

Today Naval Ravikant, a well-known Silicon valley entrepreneur, investor, and thought leader, posted this:

"The least compressible intellectual domain is physics. The least compressible artistic domain is poetry."

This is quite an insight in the era of LLM where 1 trillion parameters totaling around 4TB storage of leading models can compress the entire human knowledge, estimated to be 1-2 zettabytes (1 ZB = 1 billion TB). As a reference, we can buy a 30TB hard drive for $600 today.

Naval’s statement reflects a surprising parallel between physics and poetry, both resisting simplification due to their reliance on complex, irreducible frameworks

Physics is built on rigor, precision, and universal laws. Even seemingly simple phenomena—like a falling apple—require layers of mathematics, from Newtonian mechanics to general relativity, depending on the scale and accuracy desired. Compressing physics inevitably loses information because the world resists oversimplification; nature has an intrinsic complexity that demands detailed models. As Feynman said, "Nature cannot be fooled."

At the same time, the very language of physics—its equations—is itself already a form of extreme compression. Maxwell’s four equations capture all of classical electromagnetism, and Einstein’s E = mc² encapsulates the profound link between mass and energy. These formulas are elegant compressions of vast experimental truths, and behind each symbol lies the ultimate distillation of the knowledge and wisdom of those who came before them.

Poetry, by contrast, distills meaning, emotion, and imagery into the fewest possible words. Every word choice carries multiple layers—rhythm, sound, connotation, cultural resonance. A poem like T.S. Eliot’s The Waste Land or Li Bai’s Quiet Night Thoughts cannot be “summarized” without unraveling the layers that give them subtlety and significance. Poetry’s richness lies in its irreducibility—its power is in how it feels rather than just what it says.

At the same time, the interpretation of a poem’s exact words or verses is profoundly different from any summary. A summary captures only one possible reading, stripping away the ambiguity, music, and emotional undercurrents that make poetry alive. Each reader may uncover different meanings in the same lines—meanings shaped by personal experience, cultural context, and emotional state. This infinite interpretive space is precisely what makes poetry impossible to compress without destroying the very qualities that give it depth and resonance.

Both domains resist compression in different ways: physics because of its irreducible complexity, and poetry because of its irreducible depth.

Some truths and experiences cannot be reduced without losing their essence. If anything, both fields remind us that compression is never the full story—there’s always a residue of mystery, beauty, and meaning that escapes simplification—an irreducible core that continues to challenge both our intellect and our imagination.

核心观点

文章从纳瓦尔（Naval Ravikant）的一句话谈起：最难被压缩的知识领域是物理学，最难被压缩的艺术领域是诗歌。在大型语言模型能把人类知识“压缩”进几 TB 的时代，这句话别具深意。物理学因自然内在的复杂性而抗拒简化，而它的方程本身又是一种极致的压缩；诗歌则因不可约的深度而无法总结，任何总结都只是一种解读，会失去诗中的多义、韵律与情感。两者都提醒我们，总有一个不可压缩的核心，持续考验我们的理性与想象。

今天，著名的硅谷企业家、投资人和思想领袖纳瓦尔（Naval Ravikant）发了一条帖子：

“最难被压缩的知识领域是物理学。最难被压缩的艺术领域是诗歌。”

在这个大型语言模型（LLM）时代，这句话别具深意。如今，领先的模型拥有约 1 万亿参数，总计约 4TB 的存储空间，却能“压缩”体量约1-2 ZB（1ZB = 10亿TB）的整个人类知识。作为对比，如今我们花 600 美元就能买到一个 30TB 的硬盘。

纳瓦尔的这句话揭示了物理学与诗歌之间出人意料的相似之处：它们都依赖复杂且不可再简化的框架，从而抵抗被过度简化。

物理学建立在严谨、精确和普适规律之上。即便是看似简单的现象—比如苹果落地—也需要多个层次的数学模型来描述，从牛顿力学到广义相对论，具体取决于所需的尺度和精度。压缩物理学不可避免地会丢失信息，因为现实世界本身抗拒过度简化；自然有着内在的复杂性，必须用详尽的模型来刻画。正如费曼所说：“自然是骗不了的。”

与此同时，物理学的语言—方程式—本身就是一种极端形式的压缩。麦克斯韦的四条方程囊括了整个经典电磁学，爱因斯坦的 E = mc² 则概括了质量与能量之间深刻的联系。这些公式是对浩瀚实验真理的优雅压缩，每一个符号背后都承载着前贤们的知识与智慧的极致凝练。

相比之下，诗歌将意义、情感和意象浓缩到最少的文字之中。每一个词语的选择都承载着多重层次—节奏、声音、含义、文化共鸣。像艾略特的《荒原》或李白的《静夜思》这样的诗作，根本无法用“总结”来替代，否则会破坏那些赋予它们微妙与深意的层层意蕴。诗歌的丰富性恰恰在于它的不可约性—它的力量更多来自“让人感受到什么”，而不仅是“它在说什么”。

与此同时，诗歌中某个词句的解读，与任何总结都截然不同。总结只能捕捉一种可能的解读，剥离了诗中原有的多义、韵律与情感暗流，而这些正是诗歌鲜活的灵魂所在。不同的读者会在同一行诗句中体会到截然不同的意义—这种意义由个人经历、文化背景与情感状态共同塑造。正是这种无限的解读空间，让诗歌无法被压缩而不丧失它的深度与共鸣。

这两个领域以不同的方式抵抗压缩：物理学因为其不可约的复杂性，诗歌因为其不可约的深度。

有些真理与体验无法被简化，否则就会失去本质。物理学和诗歌都提醒我们：压缩从来不是全部的故事，总有一些神秘、美感与意义的残余无法被化约—正是这份不可压缩的核心，持续推动并考验我们的理性与想象。
